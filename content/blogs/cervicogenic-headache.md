**Cervicogenic headache** is a headache that starts in the neck but is felt in the head. With so many of us spending long hours on laptops and phones, tight neck muscles and poor posture have made it increasingly common, especially among office workers who sit in one position for hours.

It arises from the upper cervical (neck) joints, discs and muscles, which share nerve pathways with the head. Pain is referred from the neck to one or more areas of the head or face. Because it can look like a migraine or tension headache, it is often misdiagnosed.

An episode can last from an hour to several days. The pain is usually on one side of the head, on the same side as the stiffer side of the neck.

## Most Common Causes

- Poor posture, especially a forward-head position at a desk or phone
- Wear-and-tear changes in the neck joints ([cervical spondylosis](/cervical-spondylitis-physiotherapy-treatment)) and arthritis
- Neck injury such as whiplash or a sudden jolt
- Sustained stress on the neck from long static positions
- Muscle tightness and weakness around the neck and shoulders

## Symptoms of Cervicogenic Headache

- Pain on one side of the head or face, often starting at the back of the head
- A stiff neck with reduced movement
- Pain around the eye or temple on the same side
- Headache triggered by certain neck movements or sustained postures
- Pain or tenderness at the base of the skull
- Sometimes shoulder or arm discomfort on the same side

## Treatment

Treatment focuses on the neck, which is the source of the pain:

- **Physiotherapy:** manual therapy, exercise and posture correction (see below) are the mainstay of treatment.
- **Medication:** your doctor may suggest anti-inflammatory medicines or muscle relaxants for short-term relief.
- **Nerve blocks:** in persistent cases, a doctor may use an injection to relieve pain so that you can engage fully in rehabilitation.

## Physical Therapy Treatment

Research shows that physiotherapy combining manual therapy with specific exercise reduces the frequency and intensity of cervicogenic headaches, with benefits that last. Treatment has two main parts:

**1. Hands-on treatment.** Your physiotherapist uses joint mobilisation and soft-tissue techniques on the upper neck and surrounding muscles to restore movement and ease pain. High-velocity manipulation of the neck is used only after careful screening; it is not suitable for everyone, for example people with certain blood vessel problems or advanced arthritis.

**2. Exercise and posture retraining.** Your physiotherapist will set a home programme and monitor your progress. Typical exercises include:

- **Chin tucks:** gently draw your chin straight back to lengthen the back of the neck. Hold 5 seconds; repeat 10 times.
- **Deep neck flexor training:** lying on your back, gently nod and hold to strengthen the deep stabilising muscles.
- **Upper-trapezius and levator stretches:** tilt your ear towards your shoulder and hold for 20–30 seconds each side.
- **Shoulder blade squeezes:** draw your shoulder blades back and down to support better posture.

Workstation changes, such as raising your screen to eye level and taking movement breaks every 30–45 minutes, help prevent recurrence.

## When to See a Doctor Urgently

Seek medical care straight away for a sudden, severe ("worst ever") headache, a headache with fever and neck stiffness, confusion, weakness, slurred speech, vision loss, or a headache after a head injury.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I know if my headache is coming from my neck?

Cervicogenic headaches are usually one-sided, start at the back of the head or neck, and are triggered or worsened by neck movement or sustained postures. Neck stiffness on the same side is common. A physiotherapist can confirm this by examining your neck.

### How long does cervicogenic headache treatment take?

Many people notice fewer and milder headaches within 4 to 6 weeks of physiotherapy and regular exercise. Long-standing cases may need a longer programme.

### Can posture really cause headaches?

Yes. A forward-head posture increases the load on the upper neck joints and muscles, which can refer pain into the head. Correcting posture and strengthening the deep neck muscles is a key part of treatment.

### Can physiotherapy at home help cervicogenic headache?

Yes. A home visit lets the physiotherapist treat your neck and also check how you sit, work and use your phone, so the advice fits your daily routine.
