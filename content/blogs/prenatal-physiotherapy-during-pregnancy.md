Pregnancy changes almost everything about how your body moves. Your centre of gravity shifts forward, hormones such as relaxin loosen ligaments, and your abdominal and pelvic floor muscles stretch to make room for your baby. These changes are normal, but they often bring back pain, pelvic pain and fatigue.

Prenatal physiotherapy is specialised care during pregnancy that helps you manage these changes, stay safely active and prepare your body for labour and recovery. This guide explains what it involves, which problems it helps, and how to exercise safely.

## What Is Prenatal Physiotherapy?

Prenatal (antenatal) physiotherapy is treatment and exercise guidance from a physiotherapist trained in women's health. It combines:

- Assessment of posture, movement, pain and the pelvic floor
- Hands-on treatment for back, pelvic and rib pain
- A tailored, pregnancy-safe exercise plan
- Pelvic floor training
- Education on posture, lifting, sleep positions and preparing for birth

It works alongside, not instead of, your obstetrician or midwife.

## Common Problems Prenatal Physiotherapy Helps

### Low Back Pain

More than half of pregnant women experience back pain. The growing bump increases the curve of the lower back and loads the spinal joints and muscles. Physiotherapy uses gentle mobility work, core and hip strengthening and posture advice to ease it. See our guide to [physiotherapy for pregnancy back pain and cramping](/physiotherapy-for-pregnancy-back-pain-and-cramping).

### Pelvic Girdle Pain

Pain at the front of the pelvis (pubic bone) or at the back near the sacroiliac joints is common, especially when walking, climbing stairs, turning in bed or standing on one leg. Treatment includes stabilising exercises, advice on keeping the knees together during movements, and sometimes a support belt.

### Pelvic Floor Weakness and Leaking

Pregnancy places extra load on the pelvic floor muscles. Regular pelvic floor (Kegel) exercises during pregnancy reduce the risk of urinary leakage during pregnancy and after birth.

### Abdominal Separation

The two sides of the abdominal muscles naturally stretch apart as the bump grows (diastasis recti). A physiotherapist teaches you how to move, get out of bed and exercise in ways that manage load on the abdominal wall.

### Other Common Problems

- Rib and upper back pain in later pregnancy
- Carpal tunnel symptoms (tingling in the hands) from fluid retention
- Leg cramps and swelling
- Sciatic-type pain into the buttock or leg

## Benefits of Staying Active in Pregnancy

For most women with an uncomplicated pregnancy, regular moderate exercise is safe and beneficial. Health authorities including the WHO recommend about 150 minutes of moderate activity across the week. Benefits include:

- Less back and pelvic pain
- Better mood and sleep
- Lower risk of gestational diabetes and excessive weight gain
- Better stamina for labour
- Faster recovery after birth

## Safe Prenatal Exercises

Always get clearance from your doctor or midwife first, and stop if anything causes pain or feels wrong.

1. **Pelvic floor exercises:** squeeze and lift the pelvic floor as if stopping the flow of urine, hold for up to 10 seconds, then fully relax. Also practise quick squeezes. Aim for 3 sets of 10 a day.
2. **Pelvic tilts on all fours:** gently round and flatten your lower back to ease stiffness.
3. **Side-lying leg lifts and clams:** strengthen the hip muscles that support the pelvis.
4. **Wall squats or sit-to-stand:** build leg strength for daily tasks and labour positions.
5. **Walking, swimming and stationary cycling:** low-impact ways to stay fit.
6. **Breathing practice:** slow diaphragmatic breathing helps with relaxation and prepares you for labour.

## Exercise Precautions

- After the first trimester, avoid exercising for long periods lying flat on your back; use side-lying or inclined positions instead.
- Avoid contact sports, activities with a high risk of falling, scuba diving and exercise in very hot conditions.
- Stay hydrated and do not exercise to exhaustion; you should be able to talk while you exercise.

**Stop exercising and contact your doctor** if you have vaginal bleeding, fluid leaking, regular painful contractions, chest pain, dizziness or fainting, a severe headache, calf pain or swelling, or reduced baby movements.

## What Happens in a Prenatal Physiotherapy Session?

Your first session includes a discussion of your pregnancy, symptoms and goals, followed by an assessment of posture, movement and strength. Your physiotherapist then provides treatment and a home exercise plan, and reviews it as your pregnancy progresses. Later sessions can focus on labour positions, breathing and preparation for [postnatal recovery](/postnatal-physiotherapy).

## Frequently Asked Questions About Prenatal Physiotherapy

### Is prenatal physiotherapy safe during all stages of pregnancy?

Yes, prenatal physiotherapy is generally safe throughout all trimesters when performed by a qualified physiotherapist specializing in prenatal care. However, women with certain high-risk conditions or complications should always consult their obstetrician before beginning any physiotherapy program. Your physiotherapist will adapt techniques and exercises to suit your specific trimester and individual health circumstances.

### When is the best time to start prenatal physiotherapy?

While you can start prenatal physiotherapy at any point during pregnancy, many experts recommend beginning around 12-14 weeks (start of the second trimester). However, starting in the first trimester allows you to address concerns early and build a strong foundation. Even if you’re in your third trimester, it’s never too late to begin, you can still benefit from pain relief, labor preparation techniques, and postpartum planning.

### How many prenatal physiotherapy sessions will I need?

The number of sessions varies based on your individual needs, symptoms, and goals. Some women attend weekly sessions throughout pregnancy, while others may need more intensive treatment initially (2-3 times per week) then taper to biweekly or monthly maintenance. Your physiotherapist will assess your progress and adjust the frequency accordingly. Most women benefit from consistent care throughout pregnancy rather than just a few isolated sessions.

### Will I need an internal pelvic floor examination?

An internal pelvic floor assessment is often recommended but not always necessary. This examination allows your physiotherapist to accurately assess muscle strength, tone, and coordination. However, internal exams are optional and depend on your comfort level, trimester, and specific concerns. Many aspects of prenatal physiotherapy can be addressed through external assessment and exercise instruction alone. Discuss your preferences with your physiotherapist.

### Can prenatal physiotherapy help with labor and delivery?

Absolutely. Prenatal physiotherapy prepares your body for labor through pelvic floor exercises that teach muscle control and relaxation, breathing techniques for pain management, education about optimal birthing positions, and perineal massage to reduce tearing risk. Research shows these interventions can contribute to shorter labor duration and reduced need for operative deliveries.

### What should I wear to my prenatal physiotherapy session?

Wear comfortable, loose-fitting clothing that allows easy movement. Good options include maternity leggings or loose pants, a comfortable top, and supportive athletic shoes if you’ll be doing exercises. If an internal pelvic floor examination is planned, you may be asked to undress from the waist down (you’ll be properly draped). For home-based physiotherapy, you can dress in whatever makes you most comfortable.

### Is prenatal physiotherapy covered by insurance?

Coverage varies depending on your insurance provider and plan. Many health insurance plans do cover physiotherapy services, including prenatal care, when prescribed by a physician. Check with your insurance company about specific coverage details, required referrals, and any out-of-pocket costs. Even without insurance coverage, many women find the investment worthwhile given the significant benefits.

### Can I do prenatal physiotherapy if I have a high-risk pregnancy?

Women with high-risk pregnancies can often still benefit from modified prenatal physiotherapy, but this requires close collaboration with your obstetric care team. Your physiotherapist will need clearance from your obstetrician and will adapt the treatment approach to respect any restrictions or precautions related to your specific condition. In some cases, gentle exercise and positioning advice may be appropriate even when more intensive treatment isn’t.
