Plantar fasciitis is irritation and thickening of the plantar fascia, the strong band of tissue that runs along the sole of the foot from the heel bone to the toes. It is one of the most common causes of heel pain in adults, and it usually responds well to physiotherapy and simple self-care.

## Causes

Plantar fasciitis is most often caused by repetitive strain on the plantar fascia. Common contributors include:

- A sudden increase in running, walking or standing
- Worn-out or unsupportive footwear, or walking barefoot on hard floors
- Tight calf muscles and Achilles tendon
- Jumping and landing sports
- Higher body weight, which increases load through the heel
- Flat feet or very high arches

## Symptoms

- A sharp or stabbing pain under the heel or along the arch
- Pain that is worst with the first steps in the morning or after sitting, then eases slightly as you move
- Pain that builds up again after long periods of standing, walking or jumping

## Treatment

Most people improve with conservative treatment: load management, stretching and strengthening, supportive footwear or shoe inserts, and short-term pain relief. Night splints, taping and shockwave therapy can help stubborn cases. ## Physiotherapy Exercises

Do these daily. Stretch gently and hold each stretch for 20–30 seconds.

![Tennis Ball Roll](/images/blog/image003-1.webp)

### Tennis Ball Roll

Sit on a chair and roll the sole of your foot over a tennis ball (or a frozen water bottle) from heel to toes for 1–2 minutes.

![Towel Stretch](/images/blog/image005-1.webp)

### Towel Stretch

Sit with your leg straight, loop a towel around the ball of your foot and gently pull your toes towards you. Hold, then relax; repeat 3 times.

![Toe Stretch](/images/blog/image007-1.webp)

### Toe Stretch

Cross the affected foot over your other knee, hold your toes and pull them back towards your shin until you feel a stretch along the arch. Repeat 3 times.

![Toe Curls](/images/blog/image009-1.webp)

### Toe Curls

Place a small towel on the floor and scrunch it towards you with your toes. Do 10–15 curls to strengthen the small foot muscles.

![Calf Stretch](/images/blog/image011.webp)

### Calf Stretch

Stand facing a wall with the affected leg behind, heel on the floor and knee straight. Lean forward until you feel the stretch in your calf. Repeat 3 times on each side.

![Picking Up Marbles](/images/blog/image013.webp)

### Picking Up Marbles

Pick up marbles or small objects with your toes and drop them into a bowl. Aim for 15–20 pick-ups.

## Self-Care Tips

- Maintain a healthy weight to reduce stress on the plantar fascia.
- Wear supportive, cushioned shoes and replace worn-out athletic shoes.
- Avoid walking barefoot on hard floors while symptoms settle.
- Switch to lower-impact activity such as cycling or swimming for a while.
- Use ice for 15 minutes after activity to calm pain.
- Stretch your calves and arches every day.

Plantar fasciitis is diagnosed from your history and a physical examination; scans are rarely needed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does plantar fasciitis take to heal?

Most people improve significantly within 6 to 12 weeks of consistent stretching, strengthening and load management. Long-standing cases can take several months, so early treatment helps.

### Why is plantar fasciitis worse in the morning?

The plantar fascia tightens overnight while you sleep with your foot pointed down. The first steps stretch it suddenly, which causes the sharp morning pain. A few calf and towel stretches before getting out of bed can help.

### Is walking good for plantar fasciitis?

Short, comfortable walks in supportive shoes are fine, but long walks, standing for hours and running on hard surfaces can aggravate it. Reduce the load until pain settles, then build back gradually.

### Can physiotherapy cure plantar fasciitis?

Physiotherapy is a first-line treatment. Stretching, strengthening, taping, footwear advice and gradual loading resolve symptoms for most people without injections or surgery.
