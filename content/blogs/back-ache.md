Long hours at a laptop, scrolling on a phone and too little movement have made low back pain one of the most common reasons people call a physiotherapist. Most back pain is mechanical: it comes from muscles, joints and discs that are overloaded or under-used, not from serious disease. The good news is that the right movement is usually the best medicine.

This guide explains how back pain behaves, why rest alone rarely fixes it, and five simple exercises our physiotherapists use to relieve pain and stop it coming back.

## Understanding Back Pain

Back pain is usually described by how long it has lasted:

- **Acute:** less than 6 weeks
- **Sub-acute:** 6 to 12 weeks
- **Chronic:** more than 12 weeks

The most common type is non-specific (mechanical) low back pain, felt on one or both sides of the lower back and often linked to [prolonged sitting](/desk-job-syndrome-physiotherapy), poor posture, weak core muscles or a sudden awkward lift. Pain that travels down the leg below the knee may point to an irritated nerve, as in [sciatica](/sciatica-physiotherapy) or a [disc bulge](/disc-bulge-physiotherapy-treatment-l3-l4-l5).

## Why Bed Rest Is Not the Answer

Many people believe the back needs complete rest to heal. Research and clinical guidelines say the opposite: staying gently active, within comfort, leads to faster recovery and less long-term disability than bed rest. Muscles that are not used become stiff and weak, which puts more load on the joints and discs. Short breaks from aggravating activities are fine, but keep walking and moving through the day.

## 5 Exercises to Relieve Back Ache

Do these slowly and without bouncing. Mild stretching discomfort is normal; sharp or worsening pain is not, so stop and check with a physiotherapist.

### 1. Knee-to-Side Rotation Stretch

1. Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat, arms out to the sides in a "T".
2. Keeping your shoulders on the floor, slowly let both knees fall to the left.
3. Hold for 3–5 breaths, return to the centre and repeat to the right.
4. Do 5–8 slow rotations each side.

If your knees do not reach the floor, only go as far as is comfortable; range improves with practice.

### 2. Knee-to-Chest Stretch

1. Lie on your back comfortably.
2. Hold one or both knees with your hands and gently draw them towards your chest.
3. Keep your head and lower back relaxed on the floor; do not lift your head towards your knees.
4. Hold for 10–15 seconds and repeat 3–5 times.

### 3. Piriformis Stretch

The piriformis is a small muscle deep in the buttock that helps rotate the hip outwards. It tightens with long sitting, and because the sciatic nerve runs close to it, a tight piriformis can add to buttock and leg pain.

1. Lie on your back with both knees bent.
2. Cross your right ankle over your left knee.
3. Hold behind your left thigh and draw it gently towards your chest until you feel a stretch in the right buttock.
4. Hold for 30–60 seconds, then switch sides. Repeat 2–3 times per side.

### 4. Pelvic Tilt (Core Stability)

Weak deep abdominal muscles force the lower back muscles to work overtime. The pelvic tilt retrains that deep support.

1. Lie on your back with knees bent and feet flat.
2. Gently tighten your lower tummy and flatten the small of your back into the floor by tilting your pelvis.
3. Hold for 10 seconds while breathing normally; do not hold your breath.
4. Relax and repeat 10–15 times.

### 5. Cobra (Prone Press-Up)

1. Lie on your stomach with hands under your shoulders and elbows close to your body.
2. Keeping your hips on the floor and buttocks relaxed, slowly press your upper body up as far as is comfortable.
3. Hold for 2–3 breaths, then lower slowly.
4. Repeat 8–10 times. Start with a small range, or stay on your forearms, if a full press-up is too much.

## Everyday Habits That Protect Your Back

- Get up and move every 30–45 minutes when working at a desk.
- Set your screen at eye level and keep your feet supported.
- Lift with your hips and knees, keeping the load close to your body.
- Build regular walking or other [aerobic exercise](/types-of-aerobic-training-in-physiotherapy) into your week.

## When to See a Physiotherapist

Book an assessment if pain lasts more than 2–3 weeks, keeps returning, spreads into your leg, or stops you sleeping or working. A physiotherapist will find the cause and build a plan that combines manual therapy, targeted exercise and posture advice. Our [orthopaedic physiotherapy](/service/orthopaedic-physiotherapy) team treats back pain at home across Bangalore and Pune.

**Seek urgent medical care** if back pain comes with numbness around the groin or buttocks, new difficulty passing urine or controlling your bowels, weakness in the legs, fever, unexplained weight loss, or follows a fall or accident.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does back ache usually take to get better?

Most episodes of non-specific low back pain improve significantly within 2 to 6 weeks, especially when you stay active. Pain that lasts beyond 12 weeks is considered chronic and benefits from a structured physiotherapy programme.

### Should I rest or exercise when my back hurts?

Keep moving within comfort. Gentle walking and the stretches above are better than bed rest, which tends to slow recovery. Avoid only the specific movements that sharply worsen your pain until it settles.

### How often should I do these back exercises?

Once or twice a day is enough for most people. Consistency matters more than intensity: a short daily routine done for several weeks works better than an occasional long session.

### Is heat or ice better for back pain?

Ice can ease pain in the first 48 hours after a strain. After that, most people find heat more soothing because it relaxes tight muscles. Use either for 15–20 minutes with a cloth between the pack and your skin.

### Can physiotherapy at home treat back pain?

Yes. A home visit lets the physiotherapist assess how you sit, sleep and work in your own space and adjust your routine accordingly. Sessions include hands-on treatment, exercise progression and posture advice.

## Final Thoughts

Back ache is common, but it does not have to be a constant companion. Stay active, practise these five exercises regularly and fix the daily habits that load your spine.
